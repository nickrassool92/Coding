How it works

Each horse gets a score from its form: recent finishes and margins, the market's opinion of it last start, class rises and drops, weight, barrier, days since its last run, and its record at the distance, at Flemington and on today's track rating. The weights come from fitting the model on metro races in Melbourne and Sydney this year.

The honest result: the betting market predicts better than form alone, and the form model added almost nothing on top of it. So when odds are in, the market leads (sharpened slightly towards favourites, because outsiders are over-bet) and the form model only nudges it. Without odds, the form model is used on its own. Odds are gathered automatically from published markets on race morning and before the big races. Place chances come from simulating the finishing order thousands of times.

Each race predicted by a model fitted on other meetings (5-fold cross-validation). Log loss measures how good the win chances were: lower is better.
